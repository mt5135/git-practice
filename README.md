# An Article I Found Interesting

## Article

[Study: Developers are addicted to AI, and managers are making it worse](https://thenewstack.io/study-developers-are-addicted/)

## Why I Found It Interesting

What I found most interesting about this article was the way it described developers’ AI use as an “addiction” rather than a reliance as a tool. The article argues that the constant cycle of prompting and AI tool, receiving and immediate result, and trying again can create the same kind of reward system that keeps people hooked to platforms like social media or video games. Most conversations have focused on whether AI makes developers more productive or whether it will eventually replace certain parts of their jobs, but this article looks at the issue differently by asking what happens when developers become so accustomed to working with AI that they struggle to work without it. I thought the use of the word “addiction” was especially interesting because it turns something that is usually presented as technological progress into a question about our relationship with the technology itself.

I also thought it was interesting that the article does not place all of the responsibility on developers themselves, but rather argues that companies can reinforce this dependence (or “addiction”) by rewarding the people who use AI the most with raises and promotions, even while many developers report shipping code they do not fully understand. This creates a strange incentive where slowing down to understand or review AI-generated code could actually make someone appear less productive than a person generating more code quick, which makes the idea of “AI addiction” more complicated than an individual developer becoming too attached to a tool. The article suggests that the way companies measure productivity can encourage that behavior in the first place, which makes me wonder if producing more code should really be considered the same thing as being a better software engineer.
